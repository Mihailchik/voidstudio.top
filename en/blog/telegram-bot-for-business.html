A Telegram bot for business: what it can do and what the cost depends on

A Telegram bot is a program that answers people and does work on a schedule. Here is which tasks are worth handing to it and what affects the price.

What a Telegram bot can do

A bot does two things: it replies to messages and it works by itself on a schedule. Typical tasks come from that:

  • take a request or a booking and pass it to a member of staff;
  • answer frequent questions without an operator;
  • watch sources and send new items to a channel;
  • process a file: download it, convert it, send it out;
  • send a reminder, build a report, post a daily summary.

A bot fits where an action repeats the same way many times. Where a live conversation and a judgement call are needed, it only takes the request and hands it to a person.

An example: a bot that gathers podcasts

Our Podcast Bot works with nobody involved. It watches RSS feeds, YouTube channels and playlists, and Telegram channels. When a new episode comes out, the bot goes through several steps:

  1. downloads the episode;
  2. converts the audio to MP3;
  3. prepares two versions, normal and double speed;
  4. splits files that are too large into parts;
  5. sends everything to a channel and deletes temporary files.

Sources are checked on a schedule. You can also send the bot a link and it will queue it for processing. The state of every episode is written to a database, so the bot always knows what has been sent and what is still waiting.

What a bot is made of

  • The bot's code. We write in Python with the aiogram library.
  • A database. It keeps what has been done and what is in the queue. SQLite is enough for small bots.
  • A server. A bot has to run around the clock, so it needs a server. We run bots in Docker so they are easy to move and restart.
  • External services. Payments, spreadsheets, a CRM or an AI model, if the bot is connected to them.

What the cost depends on

The price of development depends not on the word "bot" but on how many scenarios it has and how many other systems it talks to. It is affected by:

  • The number of scenarios. Answering three questions and walking a person through a booking with a choice of time are different amounts of work.
  • Integrations. Every connection to payments, a CRM or a spreadsheet is a separate task with its own checks.
  • Working with files. Audio, video and large files need processing and server space.
  • An admin panel. Whether texts and settings must be editable without a developer.
  • Load. A bot for a hundred people and a bot for a hundred thousand are built differently.

After launch there are running costs: a server and maintenance. The platforms a bot works with change their rules over time, and the code has to be updated.

How to tell whether you need a bot

Count how many times a week you or your staff do the same action in the same way. If it can be described as "when this happens, do that", it can be handed to a bot. It is better to start with one such action, not with all of them at once.
